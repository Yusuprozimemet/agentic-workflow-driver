A script that runs AI coding work one step at a time

The project in graphs, each answering one question: the work on this page; the plan's yardsticks, where the time went and where each file belongs on the back page. Point at, tap or tab to any mark, and the box under its graph says the detail. Under every graph, one closed line holds its key, its lists and its exact numbers.

Day
A numbered step of the plan, written as a spec before it is built. It is not a calendar day: several Days can fit in one date.
Date
A calendar date, on the maintainer's clock.
Close
The last pull request of a Day. It ticks each promise with its evidence, and the costs are counted then.
The kit
The way of working this script automates, and the project it was started from. Its figures, reached with a person driving every step, are the baseline.
Main session
The AI conversation that does a step. The auditors and the implementer are separate AI agents it starts.
Gates
The checks work must pass: the auditors, a test seen failing, a break on purpose, the close and the evidence replay.
Hooks
Small scripts that refuse a call that breaks a rule, or cut a long output short.
The stop
A point the plan names where the work halts until the maintainer says to go on.

How to read it

The ring is the plan, drawn as one turn of a cell's cycle. It starts at the top and runs clockwise. Each segment is one Day the plan names, in the plan's order, and the arc outside a run of segments is the phase those Days belong to. A filled segment is a closed Day; a dashed one has no spec yet. A gate across the ring is a stop the plan sets: a cell does not go on past a checkpoint until it is signalled to, and this plan starts nothing after a stop until the maintainer says go on. The marker is where the work stands now. The middle says the next step in the page's own words, and how many Days are left before the next stop.

                                                How to read it

                                                Read down for the rhythm and across for one date. Each row is a date from 05:00 to 05:00 on the clock of the machine that recorded the sessions, oldest on top, so one hour is one column and work after midnight stays on the evening it belongs to. A row has five lanes. The top lane is the main session: a dark bar while it was busy, a fainter stretch inside it for a wait of a minute or longer on a command the session ran, and a thin strip over it while two main sessions were busy at once. The second lane is the sub-agents, drawn the same way. Under the sessions, each pull request runs from opened to merged, coloured by what it is, and a second pull request open at the same time is a half-height bar. Under it are its checks while they ran, red and deeper when a run failed, and in the last lane a tick at the merge, so a long coloured bar is a pull request that waited for the maintainer. A pull request opened before the row's 05:00 is cut at the row's start, and the pointer gives its whole wait. At the right of a row are that date's hours with a session busy, the hours of them spent waiting on commands, and the pull requests merged; the small bar under them draws busy and waiting on one scale for every row. Click a row and graph 10 opens below at that date, one lane per actor and every wait by its kind. An actogram is how a biologist draws an animal's activity, a row for each turn of the clock, so the rhythm shows down the page.

                                                      Where the model bends

                                                      • A file is drawn once. Many serve more than one station: CLAUDE.md is read at every one. Each hangs where it matters first.
                                                      • The whole app is one group. The driver, its hooks, rules and container are all "the protein" here. How the app itself runs is graph 11's subject.
                                                      • The guards are not CI. They stop a working session in this repository at any station. They hang beside CI's checks because both are checks by a script.
                                                      • The track-reviewer reads twice, the brief before the implementer starts and the diff after; the map draws it once, after the code.
                                                      • Length is not importance. plan.md is one middle-sized dot, and everything else is copied from it.
                                                      • The maintainer has no file. The gate in the membrane is a person.
                                                      • Nobody decides in a cell. There the steps are chemistry. Here each check is a script, a separate AI reviewer or a person, and each can say no.
