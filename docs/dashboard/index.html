A script that runs AI coding work one step at a time

The whole project in twelve graphs, each answering one question. Point at, tap or tab to any mark, and the box under its graph says the detail. Every graph keeps its exact numbers in a table under it.

Day
A numbered step of the plan, written as a spec before it is built. It is not a calendar day: several Days can fit in one date.
Date
A calendar date, on the maintainer's clock.
Close
The last pull request of a Day. It ticks each promise with its evidence, and the costs are counted then.
The kit
The way of working this script automates, and the project it was started from. Its figures, reached with a person driving every step, are the baseline.
Main session
The AI conversation that does a step. The auditors and the implementer are separate AI agents it starts.
Gates
The checks work must pass: the auditors, a test seen failing, a break on purpose, the close and the evidence replay.
Hooks
Small scripts that refuse a call that breaks a rule, or cut a long output short.
The stop
A point the plan names where the work halts until the maintainer says to go on.

How to read it

The ring is the plan, drawn as one turn of a cell's cycle. It starts at the top and runs clockwise. Each segment is one Day the plan names, in the plan's order, and the arc outside a run of segments is the phase those Days belong to. A filled segment is a closed Day; a dashed one has no spec yet. A gate across the ring is a stop the plan sets: a cell does not go on past a checkpoint until it is signalled to, and this plan starts nothing after a stop until the maintainer says go on. The marker is where the work stands now. The middle says the next step in the page's own words, and how many Days are left before the next stop.

                                                How to read it

                                                A reflex is what the body does without asking the brain. Here a step the driver finished without a park is a reflex: the signal runs from the step's start, through its sessions, to a pull request, and stays below the band. A step that parked leaves its row at the session where it parked and climbs to the band. It comes back down where the step took up again; if it never comes back, the step ended with the person.

The plan requires it at every pull request, so it is drawn the same on every row and is not a park: "without a person" here means without a park, no more. Rows run top to bottom in the order the driver started them, and the distance along a row is order, not time. The Days and the pull request numbers are those of the project the driver ran on, not this repository's.

                                                          Where the model bends

                                                          • Nobody decides in a cell. There the steps are chemistry. Here each gate is a script, a separate AI reviewer or a person, and each can say no.
                                                          • The names are the usual process. The names on the path describe the usual process for any Day. They are not a record of who did what on this one pull request.
                                                          • The check comes before the thing checked. A cell can only test a protein it has made. Here the test must fail before the code exists.
                                                          • A cell does not break its own protein to test its checks. Here the code is broken on purpose once, and the break never merges.
                                                          • A cell does not stop and wait. The driver parks and waits for a person. Nothing in a cell asks.
                                                          • Two reviewers have no biological name. The plan-auditor and the defect-rater are on the path because the existing panel has them there.
                                                          • One promise, one piece of code. A transcript is read many times and makes many copies. A promise is built once.
